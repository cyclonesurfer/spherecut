# What YouTube asks of a 360 upload, 2026-10-30

*Fetched 2026-10-30 for A1.6's `check youtube_360` (a1-plan section 6.4,
D46). Quotes are the pages' words as fetched that day. The pages change
without notice; D46 says to fetch them again before trusting a rule that
fails.*

## What the help pages say

**Upload 180- or 360-degree videos** ([1]):

- The file needs metadata, added "with an app like Adobe Premiere (2019
  or higher) or script before uploading"; the page links Google's
  spatial-media injector (release v2.0) for 360 and the VR180 apps for
  180.
- "Encode your video at a high resolution." No number is given.
- "It may take up to an hour for 360° playback to be available."
- It names no projection, aspect, size, frame rate or codec, and points to
  the upload encoding settings page ([3]) as the "advanced
  specifications".

**Use spatial audio in 360-degree and VR videos** ([2]):

- "Spatial audio uses Ambisonics (AmbiX) format", "ACN channel ordering",
  "SN3D normalisation".
- First order: "W, Y, Z, X as a 4-channel audio track", 48 kHz. With
  head-locked stereo: "W, Y, Z, X, L, R as a 6-channel audio track",
  48 kHz.
- PCM "in a MOV container"; AAC "in an MP4/MOV container", "Min. bitrate:
  256 kbps"; Opus "in an MP4 container", "Channel mapping family: 2",
  "Min. bitrate: 512 kbps" (768 kbps with head-locked stereo).
- "Only one audio track is used." "Multiple audio tracks, such as tracks
  with spatial and stereo/mono audio in the same file, are not
  supported".
- Metadata is added with the spatial-media tool (v2.1) or tools that "meet
  the YouTube spec". The page doesn't name the SA3D box; the spatial-media
  spatial audio RFC defines it.

**YouTube recommended upload encoding settings** ([3]):

- "Container: MP4", "Video codec: H.264", "Audio codec: AAC-LC or Opus or
  Eclipsa Audio", "Sample rate: 48kHz", "Chroma subsampling: 4:2:0",
  BT.709 for SDR.
- "Common frame rates include: 24, 25, 30, 48, 50, 60 frames per second
  (other frame rates are also acceptable)." "Content should be encoded and
  uploaded in the same frame rate it was recorded." "Interlaced content
  should be deinterlaced before uploading."
- Bitrate tables run from 360p to 8K (SDR at 24 to 30 fps: 80 to 160 Mbps
  at 8K, 35 to 45 at 2160p). These are recommendations, not limits.
- Nothing about 360 beyond a link to the spatial audio and 360 pages.

**Supported YouTube file formats** ([4]): .MOV, .MPEG-1, .MPEG-2, .MPEG4,
.MP4, .MPG, .AVI, .WMV, .MPEGPS, .FLV, 3GPP, WebM, DNxHR, ProRes,
CineForm, HEVC (H.265).

**Upload 3D videos** ([5]): left-right side-by-side for (flat) 3D, with
`st3d` in MOV/MP4, `StereoMode` in MKV/WebM, or frame-packing SEI; lists
"360 3D (VR videos)" as supported without a layout.

## What the metadata specifications say

**Spherical Video V1** ([6]): `ProjectionType` "Must be
`equirectangular`"; `StereoMode` "Must be `mono`, `left-right`, or
`top-bottom`"; Spherical, Stitched, StitchingSoftware and ProjectionType
required; superseded by V2.

**Spherical Video V2** ([7]): `st3d` 0 mono, 1 top-bottom (left eye on
top), 2 left-right (left eye on the left), 3 stereo-custom, 4 right-left;
`sv3d` holds `svhd` and `proj` (`prhd` plus exactly one of `equi`, `cbmp`,
`mshp`); `equi` bounds are 0.32 fixed-point crops from each edge, with
`bottom` < 0xFFFFFFFF − `top` and `right` < 0xFFFFFFFF − `left`; Matroska
has `ProjectionType` (0 rectangular, 1 equirectangular, 2 cubemap, 3 mesh)
and `ProjectionPrivate`.

## So, for `check youtube_360`

Required, because a page says the upload won't work without it:

- Spherical metadata on the video track (V1 or V2), equirectangular. No
  page says YouTube takes a V2 cubemap or mesh upload, so those fail with
  that said (a cubemap's `fix` names an equirect export).
- Spatial audio, when the file has it: ambiX (ACN, SN3D, periphonic) of
  first order in 4 channels, or 6 with head-locked stereo; 48 kHz; AAC at
  256 kbps or more (MP4/MOV), Opus at 512 (768) or more (MP4), or PCM
  (MOV). One audio track only.
- A container and video codec on the supported list ([4]).
- Progressive video ([3] says interlaced should be deinterlaced).

Recommended, which fail without making `pass` false: H.264, 4:2:0, AAC-LC
or Opus at 48 kHz, one of the common frame rates.

Not checked, since no page gives a number: resolution, aspect beyond what
the projection itself requires (the always-rules check 2:1 and 1:1),
bitrate.
